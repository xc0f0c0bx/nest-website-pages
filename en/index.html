Vacancy management. What empty space holds.

Why places stand empty

Vacancy arises when use, funds or vision are missing. Places do not stand empty because they are worthless, but because the basis for a decision is missing.

dimension missing

Ownersdo not know what is possible.

Usersdo not know that something is possible.

Administrationsees too much risk and too little evidence.

This is as true for a ground-floor shop as for an industrial site. NEST brings these sides together and supplies what a decision lacks: an opened place, data and a model that holds up.

Four steps from empty space to a place that lasts

It is a learning process. Every step builds on what the previous one has shown, and we keep collecting data throughout.

  1. 1

    Open

    The first spaces are occupied and used, starting with those that can operate straight away with little effort and have a strong public presence. Minimal investment, maximum learning.

  2. 2

    Recognise

    Operation runs under real conditions and tests the assumptions behind it. We record quantitatively and qualitatively what actually happens on site. What proves itself is stabilised.

  3. 3

    Refine

    Experience is evaluated and consolidated: on suitability of space, demand, organisational effort and economics, compared with figures from other projects. The result is a sound basis for decisions.

  4. 4

    Sustain

    Not an end point, but the transition to a long-term perspective. A place is sustained when three foundations stand: an active community that carries it, a flexible, robust structure that allows use, and an economic model that keeps it going in the long term.

Part by part

We develop larger buildings part by part. What can operate straight away opens first, and what one part shows decides the next. The phases are decision spaces, not fixed timelines: measures can be adjusted or paused at any time.

The interim use cycle

Phase zero, four steps, four tools. Every round produces data for the next: at the end the owner decides whether to extend, adjust, end or sustain. The same cycle runs in a room, a building part, a site and a neighbourhood.

  • Extend → open
  • Adjust → prepare
  • End → record
  • Sustain → permanent use

Four tools for decisions about existing buildings

BAData-based building analysis
We record, organise and assess what exists in spatial, technical, social, economic and legal terms, before use and during it, without anticipating any use. The result is evidence instead of arbitrariness.
PNPioneer use
A place is used again for the first time, with minimal but targeted adaptations and real users. Pioneer use is a methodological tool: it yields real spatial, organisational and economic insight, and it does not replace planning but prepares it.
NKUse concept and economics
Use scenarios, economic models and an impact assessment for the surroundings, continuously evaluated and compared with benchmarks from our projects. A mix of uses with different revenue logics spreads the risk and makes room for less profit-oriented formats.
DZDigital twin
A 3D scan that grows step by step into a BIM-ready working model with a facility and operating cost model. Not a snapshot, but a structure that grows with operation: the basis for cost optimisation, refurbishment and owner decisions that can be backed up.

Phase zero: record, organise, prepare

Phase zero creates the conceptual and structural conditions for a pioneer use. It starts with an open investigation: the building, its current use, the surroundings and possible perspectives are recorded, organised and assessed, without anticipating uses or building measures. After that, interventions are limited to the technical minimum, and the search for the first users begins.

At the Spinnerei Feldkirch, phase zero also runs as a research and development project in a triangle of university, NEST and real-world lab. The pioneer phase is both real-world lab and teaching material. The project is funded by the Austrian Federal Ministry for Housing, Arts, Culture, Media and Sport under its building culture funding call. TU Wien (Research Unit for Heritage Conservation and Building in Existing Contexts) and stadtland take part.

Frequently asked questions about vacancy management and interim use

What is vacancy management?

Vacancy management brings unused buildings, areas and sites back into use. It starts with an analysis of what exists, opens the place for first uses and ends with a model that holds in the long term. NEST works with a building analysis, pioneer uses, a use concept with economic assessment and a digital twin.

What is a pioneer use?

A pioneer use is the first reopening of a place, with low investment and real users. It shows what works on site before money goes into a conversion. The Spinnerei Feldkirch and the CREAU in Vienna are examples.

What happens in phase zero?

In phase zero the place is recorded, organised and assessed: the building, its current use, the surroundings and possible perspectives. Then the conditions for a pioneer use are created, with building work limited to the technical minimum. Uses or larger measures are not anticipated. At the Spinnerei Feldkirch, phase zero runs as a research project with TU Wien.

Why does NEST develop large buildings step by step?

Because every extension should rest on real experience of use. The building is divided into parts, and what can operate straight away with little effort opens first. What one part shows decides the next. Investment is targeted and risk is contained.

Does NEST also work outside Vienna?

Yes. Projects have taken place in Feldkirch, Bregenz, Andelsbuch, Schönsee, Trofaiach, Munich, Kempten and Straubing, among others.

How does NEST differ from Kreative Räume Wien?

Kreative Räume Wien is the service and advisory office for vacancy activation and interim use on behalf of the City of Vienna. NEST is a private agency. We are commissioned by owners, municipalities and developers and carry out projects, also outside Vienna and for industrial and heritage sites.

When is your task complete?

The pioneer phase does not end, it turns into long-term use. A place is sustained when three foundations stand: a social one (an active community that carries the place), an architectural one (a flexible, robust structure) and an economic one (a model that keeps the place going in the long term).

We are happy to answer further questions on costs, duration, handover and permits in conversation.

About NEST

NEST was founded in Vienna in 2015 as the first agency for vacancy management in Austria. We are architects who work with what is already there. Our aim is to change the cityscape for the better through networks, because only a lively city is a place worth living in.

Managing director is DI Lukas Böckle, shareholder DI Mag. Angelika Schmied.

We open places again for those who need them. We reduce risk through step-by-step development, with controlled experiments instead of visions from an ivory tower. We lay the groundwork for long-term economic viability and the common good.

The “Creative Au”: one hectare with three old stable buildings in the Krieau, programmed with crafts, culture and its own catering.
